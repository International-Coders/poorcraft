# Technology, Magic, and Industry

## Parallel paths

POORCRAFT 3D should support multiple deep paths rather than one mandatory
technology ladder:

- survival and exploration;
- engineering and industrial automation;
- valve-era computing and electrical control;
- magic, ritual, enchantment, and dragons;
- commerce and logistics;
- combat, factions, and rulership.

Paths may combine. The player should be able to create a magical-industrial
realm, a dragon kingdom with little machinery, a computer-focused society, or
a mostly traditional faction.

## Electrical-valve era

The industrial identity begins with electrical valves, meaning a
vacuum-tube/relay era rather than modern solid-state computing:

- thermionic valves and analog control;
- relays, switchboards, radios, and signal networks;
- early programmable or specialized computers;
- motors, generators, transformers, and storage;
- steam, hydraulic, and pneumatic machinery;
- experimental nuclear systems as the technological ceiling.

This era should be visible in architecture, sound, interfaces, NPC jobs, and
resource chains—not only in a research menu.

## Shared capability interfaces

Magic and machines should interact with common world contracts where useful:

- power and energy queries;
- item and fluid transport;
- heat, pressure, or charge;
- construction and structural support;
- sensors, signals, and automation;
- faction ownership and permissions.

The implementation must avoid forcing every system into one generic “energy”
number. A shared interface may expose multiple typed capabilities while
preserving clear rules for players.

## Content breadth rule

New machines, spells, creatures, and recipes are valuable only when they open
new play, create world consequences, or deepen a path. Do not use content
volume to hide missing NPC, settlement, or empire behavior.

## Suggested progression rhythm

| Period | Engineering expression | Magic expression | World consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early | hand tools, simple water devices, workshops | relics, basic rituals, natural sites | survive and discover |
| Growing | mills, steam, mechanical logic | schools, wards, creature bonds | specialize and help a place |
| Established | generators, valves, control networks | advanced travel, enchantment, territorial magic | operate a settlement |
| Realm-scale | infrastructure, early computers, risky nuclear systems | dragon pacts, grand rituals, magical defenses | reshape power and politics |

This is a design rhythm, not a mandatory linear tech tree. It gives the player
clear reasons to return to the world at increasing scales.

## Computer gameplay rule

Valve-era computers should control visible systems: routing water, managing
signals, gates, alarms, production, research instruments, communications, or
defenses. They must be understandable through physical inputs/outputs and
debug tools. Avoid inventing a computer system that is only a difficult coding
minigame with no world consequence.
