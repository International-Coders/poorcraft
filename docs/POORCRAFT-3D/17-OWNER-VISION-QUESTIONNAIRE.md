# Owner Vision Questionnaire

These questions are not a test. They are the decisions that determine whether
POORCRAFT 3D becomes the game you imagine or merely a technically impressive
collection of systems. You can answer in plain language, by number, or over
several conversations.

## Answers already received

| Question area | Owner direction recorded |
|---|---|
| Best ten minutes | Read a seed and choose the best personal place to begin, in the spirit of a good sandbox-world start. |
| Narrative | The seed has lore/factions and old conditions, but the player makes the important new history and may stay independent. |
| Empire UI | Directly manage one capital through the player’s visible city plus an oversight panel; NPCs request improvements and needs. |
| World shape | Seed-driven biomes determine mountains, coasts, beaches, plains, forests, river corridors, and related opportunities. |
| Power balance | No strongest branch; powerful systems such as nuclear power have world risks and requirements. |
| Beta requirements | Empires, broad building/crafting/castles, NPCs/city systems, and strong biome/world quality. |
| Expansion | Friendly cities may retain faction identity as autonomous puppet/protectorate states; detailed management stays focused on the main city. |
| Warfare | First-person is the primary experience; NPCs receive high-level orders rather than RTS micro-control. |
| Construction | Personal building is player-created; civic construction can be player-built or completed by NPC builders after a real project commitment. |
| Capital travel | Seed locations are constrained to roughly 10–25 minutes of walking between major capitals. |
| New factions | Player factions are allowed but must choose an ideology based on existing faction values. |
| NPC autonomy | Karma determines whether NPCs help, refuse, leave, betray, or rebel. |
| Server scale | Host chooses limits; 128 players is a target when measured server capacity supports it. |
| Karma foundation | Each faction has its own karma baseline, interpreted through city and personal experience. |
| Ideology evolution | A player chooses an initial ideology, then evolves it through play. |
| Conquered cities | They grow as autonomous puppet states unless the player releases them. |
| City anchors | Player-built cities require Bed, Work, and Idle boxes for NPCs. |
| Death | Named NPC deaths are permanent, reducing city capability and garrison strength. |

## A. The feeling of play

1. When you picture the best ten minutes of POORCRAFT 3D, what is the player
   doing, seeing, and deciding?
2. Is the tone mostly hopeful adventure, dark survival, political intrigue,
   comedy/sandbox chaos, or a deliberate mixture? Which should dominate?
3. Should the player feel powerful early, or begin weak and earn influence
   slowly?
4. How punishing should death, hunger, weather, and loss of equipment be?
5. Is first-person immersion always central, or may empire play include a
   strategic map/overhead planning mode?

## B. Player freedom and progression

6. Should every player eventually be able to master magic and technology, or
   should choices create meaningful limits and tradeoffs?
7. Which path deserves the best/most content first: magic, engineering,
   exploration/combat, commerce, or rulership?
8. What makes a player feel they have “won” a solo world: personal mastery,
   founding a town, controlling a faction, building an empire, defeating a
   threat, or setting their own goal?
9. Should there be a main narrative spine, or only emergent faction/world
   stories with optional authored arcs?
10. What should prevent the player from becoming an unstoppable ruler too
    quickly: resources, legitimacy, enemies, NPC trust, logistics, danger,
    time, or something else?

## C. Empire and factions

11. Does the player rule directly, appoint NPC leaders, give high-level
    policies, command armies, or use a mixture of these?
12. Should wars be personally fought in first person, strategically commanded,
    automatically resolved from preparation, or mixed?
13. Can the player found an entirely new faction, or only rise within/capture
    existing factions during beta?
14. Are castles primarily cities, military forts, noble seats, magical sites,
    industrial centers, or different by faction?
15. How large should the player’s empire become in a normal solo save: one
    stronghold, several towns, a region, or most of the world?

## D. NPC life

16. Which NPC behavior would make you say “this game is alive”:
    relationships, work, conversations, fear, politics, building, family,
    trade, war, or something else?
17. How much direct control should the player have over recruited NPCs?
18. Should NPCs be allowed to make major decisions that surprise or oppose the
    player, including leaving, betraying, or founding a rival group?
19. Are named companions rare story characters, recruitable specialists, or a
    broad settlement population?
20. What consequences should crime, cruelty, generosity, and promises have?

## E. World and terrain

21. What visual balance feels right: clearly blocky construction in natural
    landscapes, nearly smooth terrain with block building, or a more stylized
    middle point?
22. How much can players reshape mountains, rivers, and caves before the world
    should resist or require large effort?
23. Should terrain be mostly realistic geography, highly fantastical floating/
    magical forms, or both in distinct regions?
24. How long should it take on foot to travel between major capitals? What
    kinds of travel—mounts, dragons, ships, portals, trains—should alter that?
25. What should a player most often find underground: resources, ruins,
    dungeons, magic, creatures, hidden settlements, or all of these?

## F. Technology and magic

26. What does a valve-era computer let a player do that feels fun and visible?
27. How grounded versus wild should nuclear technology be, and what risks or
    benefits should it bring to the world?
28. What is magic’s emotional identity: mysterious, dangerous, scholarly,
    natural, religious, industrialized, or chaotic?
29. What role should dragons play: mount, companion, enemy, faction power,
    rare world event, or all of the above?
30. Which machine/magic combination would make you happiest to build or see?

## G. Multiplayer and ownership

31. What is the intended typical group size: solo, two friends, four friends,
    a small server, or a large community?
32. Should friends jointly rule one settlement, create rival realms, or both?
33. What kinds of griefing, theft, war, or territory conflict are acceptable
    in hosted worlds?
34. Is the first public release focused on private co-op, persistent servers,
    or a combination?

## H. Boundaries

35. Which three features must be in the first beta even if other features are
    cut?
36. Which tempting features should be explicitly delayed to protect the core?
37. What games, art styles, or design directions should POORCRAFT 3D avoid?
38. What would make you personally say: “This is no longer my game”?

## How answers become decisions

After answers are received, write each accepted choice as an explicit decision
with its reason, affected systems, and what it rules out. Then update the work
breakdown before any related implementation begins.
