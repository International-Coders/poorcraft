# Cosmology — Magic, the Old Powers, and the Nature of the Ruin

## What magic actually is (in-world)

Magic in Valdenmoor is not divine or supernatural — it is a real, material
property of the world. The Ember Covenant's term for it is **Anima** —
the baseline energetic flow of living things, stone resonance, and heat.
Anyone can feel it if they pay attention; very few can shape it
deliberately. This is why:

- Mages are not born, they are trained. There are no "chosen ones."
- Magic is tiring and resource-consuming (mana as a real depletion stat).
- Magic does not violate physics — it accelerates, concentrates, or
  redirects natural forces. A fire spell is not "conjuring fire from
  nothing"; it is concentrating ambient heat into a point rapidly enough
  to combust.
- Industrial machinery and magic are not opposites — both are methods of
  controlling energy flow, which is why the Ironborn and the Ember
  Covenant have had both conflicts and partnerships over the centuries.

## The Old Powers

Before Era I, the world was home to entities the Ashen Order calls
"the Old Powers" — beings or forces of concentrated Anima that predate
recorded history. They are not gods in the religious sense; they left
no temples, no dogma, and no surviving records of intentional
communication with humans. What they left:

- **Geode formations** — concentrated Anima crystallized into stone.
  The Geode Guardian is an Anima-construct that forms around a
  particularly large geode. It is not malicious; it is a natural defense
  mechanism, the same way a crab has a shell.
- **The Null** — a region or force associated with the Null Knight.
  The Ashen Order believes the Null is what happens when Anima is
  completely drained from an area — a negative-energy zone that animates
  a protector. The Null Knight is not undead; it is an Anima-vacuum
  construct. Fighting it well requires understanding this distinction
  (this is future boss-mechanic design territory).
- **Dragon resonance** — dragons are the largest living concentrators of
  Anima in the world. They do not breathe fire by combusting fuel; they
  release concentrated Anima that superheats the air. This is why
  dragonfire and regular fire behave differently (a design note for the
  magic system: dragonfire ignores fire-resistance but can be partially
  absorbed by an Anima-attuned mage).

## The Ruin and the Anima breach

The Ashen Order's best (private, not publicly shared) hypothesis about
the Ruin: the original Ashborn mine in Era II reached a deep layer of
hyper-concentrated Anima — essentially a geode the size of a mountain.
The mining destabilized it. The Ember Covenant's early mages (who existed
then, barely) tried to redirect the Anima release and partially succeeded
— but the partial success meant the Anima dispersed into the environment
rather than exploding outward, causing the multi-decade ecological
disruption of the Long Winter instead of a single catastrophic event.

This hypothesis is never stated in the game as fact. The player can
piece it together from: an Ashen Order lore book fragment, a piece of
Ironborn oral history, and a Covenant shrine inscription. This is a
"hidden lore trail" design — the world rewards curious players.

## What spells actually do (mechanical grounding for the magic system)

Each spell type maps to a real Anima manipulation:

- **Damage spells** — rapid Anima concentration at a target point,
  producing heat/concussive force.
- **Movement spells** — Anima-assisted kinetic push on the caster's own
  mass (a blink/leap is not teleportation; it is a very fast push).
- **Ward spells** — a sustained Anima field around the caster that
  dissipates incoming concentrated Anima (i.e., works against magic-
  adjacent attacks; less effective against a plain sword).
- **Crafting-utility spells** — precise, low-power Anima flow directed
  into a material to heat/shape/light it. A smelt spell is literally what
  a furnace does, but in your hands, slower and more expensive in mana.

## The enchanting economy (magic + industry)

An enchanted tool is a tool whose material has been treated with a
controlled Anima-saturation process — the smithing minigame already
exists as the manufacturing step; enchanting is the Anima-treatment
step layered on top. This grounds the enchanting minigame in the same
cosmology without requiring a separate magical economy: you still need
the iron, you still need the forge, and then you need the Covenant
ritual on top. This is why Artisan-path players and Battlemage-path
players can both produce enchanted items via different routes, and why
the Ironborn faction is interested in (but not fully trusting of) the
Covenant.
