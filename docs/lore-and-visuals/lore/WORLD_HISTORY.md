# World History — The Realm of Valdenmoor

## The Four Eras (the in-world calendar)

All in-game chronicle entries, lore books, and NPC dialogue reference
dates as "Era X, Year Y." The player's game always begins at Era IV,
Year 1 — the opening of the Age of Reckoning.

---

### Era I — The Age of First Flame (≈ 800 years before the player)

The world was settled by two root peoples arriving from opposite coasts:
the **Alder-kin** (builders, farmers, river-settlers — ancestor culture
of The Free Holds and The Accord) and the **Ashborn** (miners, smiths,
volcano-coast dwellers — ancestor culture of The Ironborn). They coexisted
uneasily across the central plains.

**Key events:**
- **E1Y12 — The Ashenmoor Compact**: the first formal trade agreement
  between the Alder-kin and the Ashborn, struck at a crossing of two
  rivers, creating the settlement of Ashenmoor (now the Accord's seat).
- **E1Y214 — The Great Smelter of Ashenmoor**: the Ashborn's masterwork,
  a multi-floor industrial forge built into the cliffside above Ashenmoor.
  Still partially standing in the game world — the Ironborn revere it.
- **E1Y301 — The First Mages**: isolated scholars in the highland forests
  began formalizing knowledge of natural energy flow — not "magic" yet,
  just careful observation of heat, plant growth, and stone resonance.
  The seed of what became the Ember Covenant.

---

### Era II — The Age of Ruin (≈ 500 years before the player)

A prolonged period of ecological and political collapse. The exact cause
is disputed (the player learns conflicting accounts from different factions,
which is intentional — this ambiguity is a design feature):

- **Accord NPCs** believe the Ruin was caused by the Ashborn over-mining
  a deep ore seam and releasing something unstable from beneath.
- **Ironborn NPCs** believe the Ruin was a natural catastrophe weaponized
  by early mages who panicked and made it worse.
- **Ember Covenant NPCs** believe the Ruin was a spiritual event — the
  world "exhaling" after centuries of being wounded — and that the Covenant's
  founding was the world's own attempt to heal itself.
- **Ashen Order NPCs** believe none of the above and are actively trying
  to find the real answer — which is why they exist.

**Key events:**
- **E2Y1 — The Sundering**: a catastrophic event (described differently
  by each faction) that destroyed the original settlements of Ashenmoor's
  upper city and scattered both root peoples.
- **E2Y80 — The Long Winter**: a multi-decade cold period following the
  Sundering. The mushroom forests (mushroom-cap biome) grew explosively
  during this era — the Covenant believes this was the world healing.
- **E2Y200 — The Wandering Century**: nomadic era, no fixed governance.
  The Nameless as a cultural identity (those who rejected any authority
  after the Ruin) formed during this period.
- **E2Y340 — The Ember Covenant Founded**: scholars and druidic survivors
  formed a formal order in the highland forests, built the first Covenant
  shrines. They had real functional magic by this point.
- **E2Y410 — The Ironborn Guild Chartered**: the Ashborn remnants formally
  reorganized as a craft-and-trade guild rather than a people, deliberately
  choosing industrial identity over ethnic identity. They rebuilt the Great
  Smelter.

---

### Era III — The Age of Rebuild (≈ 200 years before the player)

The world slowly recovers. New settlements, new political structures, and
the gradual re-accumulation of technological and magical knowledge.

**Key events:**
- **E3Y1 — The Accord of Ashenmoor**: representatives of the Alder-kin
  descendants, the Ironborn Guild, and the Ember Covenant signed a formal
  governance compact. The Accord (faction) takes its name from this
  document. The Free Holds refused to sign — their founding act of
  independence.
- **E3Y55 — The Ashen Archive Founded**: neutral scholars, dissatisfied
  with each faction's politicized version of history, established an
  independent library in a marble outcrop in the central highlands.
  The Ashen Order was born.
- **E3Y120 — The Nameless Raids**: a violent escalation of bandit and
  rogue activity that threatened to undo the Rebuild. The Accord's first
  military response. The Nameless formally coalesced as a counter-identity
  to the Accord.
- **E3Y180 — The Great Map**: the Ashen Order completed the first
  comprehensive map of the known world. A copy exists in every Ashen
  Library worldgen structure.
- **E3Y220 — The Coal Compact**: the Ironborn and the Accord jointly
  agreed that coal extraction (and later, oil and nuclear resources) would
  require joint authorization. This is the political root of why the
  Ironborn is not a subservient faction — they have real veto power over
  industrial expansion.

---

### Era IV — The Age of Reckoning (the player's era)

The player begins at Era IV, Year 1. The world is stable but tense. The
Accord's authority is contested by a newly assertive Free Holds, the
Ember Covenant has recently rediscovered a fragment of pre-Ruin magic
that makes the other factions nervous, and the Nameless have a new
organizer (never named in the game — intentionally mysterious).

The player's story is their own, written by the chronicle as they play.
The faction system exists to give that story political stakes — every
significant choice the player makes reflects on the world's ongoing
balance, even if the player doesn't think of it as "political gameplay."

## Canon rules for all lore book text and NPC dialogue

1. Every faction believes it is right and has a genuine, internally
   coherent ideology — no faction is purely evil except in its own
   opponents' eyes. Even the Nameless has a coherent (if destructive)
   philosophy about the failure of all compacts.
2. The cause of the Ruin is deliberately ambiguous and unresolved in the
   game. No faction book or NPC definitively resolves it — that is a long-
   term mystery for the chronicle to reference but never answer.
3. All named events and places above are canonical. New quest text and
   NPC dialogue must reference them accurately — do not contradict a
   named date or event.
4. The player's character has no fixed origin or identity — they are
   described in lore books and chronicle entries as "the newcomer" or
   "the wanderer" or by a title they earn through the quest/faction system.
